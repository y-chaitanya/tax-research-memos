# Memorandum 6 — California Economic Nexus for an Out-of-State Online Seller

**Prepared by:** Chaitanya Yarlagadda, EA
**Date:** October 2026
**Tax years:** 2025 and 2026

---

## Facts

Client is an online retailer organized and located in Oregon. It sells furniture
(tangible personal property) to customers nationwide and ships orders from its
Oregon warehouse by common carrier. It has no employees, offices, inventory or
representatives in California, and no related entities.

Sales delivered to California customers:

| Period | Own website | Through an online marketplace | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | $420,000 | $150,000 | $570,000 |
| 2026, January through September | $310,000 | — | $310,000 |

The client is not registered with the California Department of Tax and Fee
Administration (CDTFA) and has not collected any California tax.

## Issue

Is the client a "retailer engaged in business in this state" required to
register with the CDTFA and collect California use tax on its sales delivered
into California, and if so, from when?

## Rule

**Constitutional standard.** *South Dakota v. Wayfair, Inc.*, 585 U.S. 162
(2018), overruled the physical-presence requirement of *Quill Corp. v. North
Dakota*, 504 U.S. 298 (1992). A state may require an out-of-state seller with no
physical presence to collect tax where the seller has a substantial economic
connection with the state.

**California economic nexus.** Effective April 1, 2019, Revenue and Taxation Code
§6203 treats as a retailer engaged in business in California any retailer that,
in the preceding or current calendar year, has total combined sales of tangible
personal property for delivery in California by the retailer and all persons
related to the retailer exceeding **$500,000**. Such a retailer must register
with the CDTFA and collect use tax. California did not adopt a separate
transaction-count test.

**District taxes.** Revenue and Taxation Code §7262 applies a parallel $500,000
threshold for collection of district use taxes.

**Physical presence still counts.** §6203 independently treats a retailer as
engaged in business in California if, for example, it maintains property or
inventory in the state. The $500,000 threshold matters only to a seller with no
physical presence.

**Marketplace sales.** Under the Marketplace Facilitator Act (Revenue and
Taxation Code §§6040–6049.5), a registered marketplace facilitator is treated as
the retailer for, and collects tax on, the sales it facilitates.

## Analysis

The client has no physical presence in California, so its obligation turns
entirely on the $500,000 economic threshold.

**Direct sales alone do not reach it.** Website sales were $420,000 in 2025 and
$310,000 in 2026 through September. Neither year exceeds $500,000 on direct
sales alone.

**The answer depends on the marketplace sales.** If the $150,000 of 2025
marketplace sales counts toward the client's "total combined sales," 2025
combined sales were $570,000. Because the statute looks to the preceding *or*
current calendar year, the client would then have been required to register and
collect use tax on its own direct sales throughout 2026, including the $310,000
already made without collecting. If the marketplace sales do not count, the
client has not crossed the threshold in either year and has no current
obligation.

The two readings lead to opposite results, and the statutory language quoted in
the CDTFA's published guidance does not by itself resolve which applies. That is
the controlling open question in this memorandum, and **I have not reached a
conclusion on it.**

**Two things are clear either way.**

- The marketplace facilitator, not the client, is responsible for collecting tax
  on the $150,000 of facilitated sales.
- The client is close to the threshold on direct sales alone. More than $190,000
  of additional direct California sales in 2026 would carry it past $500,000
  without regard to the marketplace question.

## Conclusion

The client has no physical presence in California, and its direct sales have not
exceeded $500,000 in either year. Whether it has been required to register and
collect California use tax since January 1, 2026 depends on whether its 2025
marketplace sales count toward the threshold.

- **If they count:** the client has an uncollected liability on 2026 direct
  sales and should register with the CDTFA promptly.
- **If they do not:** the client has no current obligation but should track
  direct California sales monthly for the rest of 2026.

## What would change the answer

- Any inventory stored in California, including inventory placed in a
  marketplace's fulfillment warehouse, would create physical presence and an
  obligation regardless of the threshold.
- A related entity with California sales would be combined with the client's
  sales for the threshold.
- Authority confirming that facilitated sales are included in, or excluded from,
  the threshold would settle the open question.

## Open items

- Obtain the current text of Revenue and Taxation Code §6203 and any CDTFA
  regulation or publication addressing whether marketplace sales count toward a
  remote seller's $500,000 threshold
- Obtain monthly 2025 and 2026 California sales by channel to confirm the totals
  above
- Confirm with the marketplace that it is registered with the CDTFA and collected
  tax on the client's California sales
- Determine whether the client participates in any fulfillment program that
  places inventory in California
- Out of scope: California franchise and income tax nexus, which is governed by
  separate rules (Revenue and Taxation Code §23101) and by Public Law 86-272
