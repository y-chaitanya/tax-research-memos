# Memorandum 1 — Repair vs. Improvement: Rental Property Expenditures

**Prepared by:** Chaitanya Yarlagadda, EA
**Date:** September 2026
**Tax year:** 2026

---

## Facts

Client owns a residential rental duplex placed in service in 2015. Unadjusted basis in the building is $420,000. The property was held for rental use throughout 2026.

Expenditures during 2026:

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Interior repainting between tenancies | $1,900 |
| Replacement of HVAC condenser and evaporator coil after failure | $6,800 |
| Full roof replacement following storm damage (insurance claim filed) | $14,500 |

The HVAC work replaced roughly half of the system's components. The remaining components stayed in service.

## Issue

Which of these expenditures are currently deductible repairs under §162, and which must be capitalized as improvements under §263(a) and Reg. §1.263(a)-3?

## Rule

Amounts paid to keep property in ordinary, efficient operating condition are deductible under §162. Amounts paid to improve a unit of property must be capitalized under §263(a).

Reg. §1.263(a)-3(d) defines an improvement as an amount paid for a **betterment**, a **restoration**, or an **adaptation** of the unit of property to a new or different use.

For buildings, Reg. §1.263(a)-3(e)(2) requires the improvement analysis to be applied separately to the building structure and to each of nine enumerated building systems, including the HVAC system. The comparison is to the system, not to the building as a whole.

Reg. §1.263(a)-3(k)(1) treats as a restoration, among other things, the replacement of a part that is a major component or substantial structural part of the unit of property, and the restoration of property after a casualty for which a loss was deducted or basis was adjusted.

Three provisions may permit a current deduction for an amount that would otherwise be capitalized:

- **De minimis safe harbor**, Reg. §1.263(a)-1(f)
- **Routine maintenance safe harbor**, Reg. §1.263(a)-3(i) — for buildings, activities expected to be performed more than once during a ten-year period
- **Safe harbor for small taxpayers**, Reg. §1.263(a)-3(h) — available where total amounts paid during the year for repairs, maintenance, and improvements on the building do not exceed the lesser of 2% of unadjusted basis or $10,000

## Analysis

### Repainting — $1,900

Repainting between tenancies restores no major component, adds no capacity, and does not adapt the property to a new use. It keeps the unit in ordinary rentable condition. Deductible under §162.

### HVAC condenser and coil — $6,800

This is the judgment item in the engagement.

Because HVAC is analyzed as its own unit of property under Reg. §1.263(a)-3(e)(2), the relevant comparison is not the $420,000 building. It is the HVAC system alone. Measured that way, a condenser and evaporator coil together are a significant portion of the system.

**Argument for capitalization.** The condenser and coil perform a discrete and critical function within the HVAC system. Replacing both may constitute a major component under Reg. §1.263(a)-3(k)(6), making the cost a restoration.

**Argument for deduction.** The system was not replaced in its entirety. Ductwork, the air handler, controls, and distribution components continue in service. The replacement restored the system to its prior operating condition rather than improving it.

The routine maintenance safe harbor does not apply. This was a response to a failure, not an activity the client reasonably expected to perform more than once in a ten-year period.

**Recommendation.** Obtain the contractor's scope of work identifying exactly which components were replaced and what share of the system they represent. Document the conclusion and its basis either way. On these facts, absent documentation supporting a narrower reading, restoration treatment is the more defensible position.

### Roof replacement — $14,500

Capitalize, for two independent reasons.

First, a roof is a major component of the building structure. Replacing it in full is a restoration under Reg. §1.263(a)-3(k)(1)(vi).

Second, if a casualty loss is deducted or basis is adjusted for the storm damage, Reg. §1.263(a)-3(k)(1)(i) independently requires restoration treatment. The amount capitalized under that provision is limited by reference to the basis adjustment. Insurance proceeds must be coordinated with the casualty loss computation under §165 before the capitalized amount is determined.

### Small taxpayer safe harbor

Unadjusted basis is $420,000. Two percent is $8,400, which is less than the $10,000 ceiling, so the applicable limit is $8,400.

Total 2026 amounts paid for repairs, maintenance, and improvements are $23,200. That exceeds $8,400, so the safe harbor is not available for this property this year.

Worth noting: the test aggregates all such amounts for the building. It is not applied item by item. A client who asks "can I just expense the small ones under the safe harbor" is asking the wrong question once the annual total is over the limit.

## Conclusion

1. **Deduct** the $1,900 repainting as a repair under §162.
2. **Capitalize** the $14,500 roof as a restoration, and coordinate the capitalized amount with the casualty loss and insurance proceeds.
3. **Treat the $6,800 HVAC cost as a restoration** unless the contractor's documentation supports that the replaced parts were not a major component of the HVAC system. Document the basis for the position taken either way.
4. The small taxpayer safe harbor is unavailable for 2026 because total amounts exceed 2% of unadjusted basis.

## What would change the answer

- If the HVAC work had been a service call or a component replacement expected more than once in ten years, the routine maintenance safe harbor could apply.
- If total 2026 building expenditures had been under $8,400, the small taxpayer safe harbor would allow current deduction of the entire amount.
- If no casualty loss is claimed and no basis adjustment is made for the storm damage, the roof would be analyzed solely as a major component replacement, which reaches the same result here but on one ground rather than two.

## Open items

- Contractor scope of work for the HVAC replacement
- Insurance settlement documentation and the casualty loss computation
- Confirmation of current de minimis safe harbor threshold and whether the client has a written capitalization policy in place
