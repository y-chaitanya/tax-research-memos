## Facts

The taxpayer is a single individual residing in California. His income consists of a primary W-2 position supplemented by a side business. He owes an aggregate federal balance of $30,000 across tax years 2023 and 2024. All required returns have been timely and accurately filed.

Monthly income is $5,800 against IRS allowable national and local standard living expenses of $5,100, leaving monthly net disposable income of $700. His asset profile consists of $2,000 in vehicle equity, $1,500 in liquid bank balances, and a 401(k) retirement account with a stated balance of $10,000. Whether the plan permits withdrawal during employment has not been confirmed. He has no penalties assessed for tax years 2020 through 2022. He has recently received Notice LT11, the Final Notice of Intent to Levy and Notice of Your Right to a Hearing.

## Issue

Which IRS administrative collection alternative matches the taxpayer's actual ability to pay, and what procedural steps must be taken immediately to prevent a levy?

## Rule

**1. Installment Agreement.** Under IRC § 6159 and IRM 5.14, the IRS may enter written agreements for payment of tax liabilities in installments. Balances at or below $50,000 may qualify for streamlined processing, generally allowing repayment over 72 months or through the collection statute expiration date, whichever is earlier, without full financial disclosure on Form 433-A. The $50,000 figure is an administrative threshold set by the Internal Revenue Manual, not a statutory ceiling.

**2. Offer in Compromise.** Under IRC § 7122 and IRM 5.8, the IRS may compromise a liability on grounds of doubt as to collectibility. The minimum acceptable offer must equal or exceed the taxpayer's Reasonable Collection Potential, calculated as the net realizable equity in assets plus future disposable income: twelve months for a lump-sum offer, twenty-four months for a periodic payment offer.

Retirement accounts are not included in RCP at their stated balance. Under IRM 5.8.5, they are valued at the amount the taxpayer could actually withdraw, reduced by the income tax and the ten percent early withdrawal penalty that liquidation would trigger. Where the plan does not permit withdrawal during employment, the account may carry no realizable equity at all.

**3. Currently Not Collectible.** Under IRM 5.16, a case may be placed in CNC status where collection would cause economic hardship, that is, where no disposable income remains after allowable living expenses.

**4. Collection Statute Expiration Date.** Under IRC § 6502, the IRS generally has ten years from the date of assessment to collect.

**5. Collection Due Process Hearing.** Under IRC § 6330, a taxpayer has thirty days from the date of an LT11 to request a Collection Due Process hearing on Form 12153. A timely request suspends levy action and permits the taxpayer to propose collection alternatives before the IRS Independent Office of Appeals. It also suspends the running of the collection statute while the hearing and any appeals are pending, and the statute cannot expire before the ninetieth day after the final determination (IRC § 6330(e)(1)).

**6. Penalty Rate Mitigation.** Under IRC § 6651(h), the failure-to-pay rate is reduced from 0.5% to 0.25% per month while an approved installment agreement is in effect, provided the return was filed timely.

## Analysis

**Currently Not Collectible.** CNC is inappropriate. The taxpayer has documented monthly net disposable income of $700 after allowable standard expenses. Because he has an immediate ability to pay without economic hardship, CNC would be rejected by Collections.

**Offer in Compromise.** The RCP calculation requires correcting the retirement account to its realizable value. At a combined federal and California marginal rate of roughly thirty percent plus the ten percent early withdrawal penalty, a $10,000 balance yields net realizable equity of approximately $6,000, and nothing at all if the plan bars withdrawal during employment.

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Vehicle equity | $2,000 |
| Bank balances | $1,500 |
| 401(k), net of tax and penalty | ~$6,000 |
| Future income (12 × $700, lump sum) | $8,400 |
| **Reasonable Collection Potential** | **~$17,900** |

RCP of roughly $17,900 sits well below the $30,000 liability, and on that figure alone a doubt-as-to-collectibility offer would appear viable. It is not, and the reason is the limitation built into the formula itself: RCP measures future income over twelve months for a lump-sum offer, while the IRS retains the full collection period in which to collect. Over seventy-two months the same $700 yields $50,400, more than enough to retire the $30,000 balance with accruing interest. The IRS generally will not accept an offer when it believes the liability can be paid in full as a lump sum or through a payment agreement (Form 656 Booklet; IRM 5.8.4), so an offer here would be rejected.

**Streamlined Installment Agreement.** The taxpayer satisfies the streamlined parameters. The balance is within the $50,000 administrative threshold, and $700 per month retires $30,000 well inside seventy-two months.

**Procedural preservation.** Because an LT11 has issued, enforcement is imminent. Filing Form 12153 within the thirty-day window suspends levy action and establishes a forum before Appeals in which to propose the installment agreement. The cost of that step is that the collection statute is suspended while the hearing is pending. Here that cost is immaterial, because the taxpayer will full-pay well inside the remaining period; for a taxpayer closer to the CSED it could be decisive, and should be weighed rather than assumed.

**Penalty relief.** First-Time Abate under IRM 20.1.1.3.3.2.1 applies to the earliest qualifying period only, here 2023, and is supported by the clean compliance history for 2020 through 2022. Timing matters: the failure-to-pay penalty continues to accrue until the balance is paid, so a request made when the agreement is first established would abate only the penalty accrued to that date. Requesting FTA after the balance is satisfied captures the full amount.

## Conclusion

File Form 12153 within thirty days of the LT11 date to request a Collection Due Process hearing. Within that forum, propose a direct-debit streamlined installment agreement at $700 per month. An Offer in Compromise should not be pursued; the taxpayer's full-pay capacity within the collection period makes rejection near-certain. Once the balance is satisfied, request First-Time Abate for 2023 to remove the failure-to-pay penalty in full.

## What would change the answer

- **If the 401(k) cannot be accessed during employment.** Net realizable equity falls further and RCP drops accordingly. It would not change the recommendation, because the OIC fails on full-pay capacity rather than on RCP, but it would change the offer figure if circumstances later made an offer viable.
- **If the W-2 position ends or the side business income proves irregular.** Disposable income is the entire basis for the recommendation. A sustained drop toward zero moves the case from installment agreement to CNC, and may make an OIC viable for the first time.
- **If a 2024 balance is still accruing or returns for later years are unfiled.** Streamlined treatment depends on the aggregate assessed balance, and an installment agreement defaults if the taxpayer falls behind on current obligations.
- **If the taxpayer is not current on 2026 estimated payments.** Failure to remain current defaults an existing agreement. This is the most common cause of a resolved case reopening.
- **If the thirty-day CDP window has already lapsed.** An equivalent hearing remains available but does not suspend levy action and carries no right to judicial review.

## What remains open

- **The assessment dates and CSEDs have not been verified.** Account transcripts for 2023 and 2024 are required before any agreement is proposed, both to confirm the collection period and to fix the thirty-day CDP deadline from the actual LT11 date.
- **The taxpayer's marginal rate has not been determined.** The retirement account valuation above uses an estimated combined rate. The actual figure depends on his bracket and California treatment and should be computed before an RCP figure is relied upon.
- **The vehicle equity has not been tested against the allowances in IRM 5.8.5**, which may reduce the amount included in RCP.
- **Whether the side business generates deductible expenses** not reflected in the stated $5,800 monthly income has not been examined. This affects both disposable income and the Form 433 presentation if full disclosure later becomes necessary.
