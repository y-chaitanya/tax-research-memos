## How I approach a research question

1. **Pin down the facts that matter.** Most of what a client tells you is background. A few details decide the answer.
2. **Start with the primary source.** Code section first, then regulations, then IRS guidance. A publication is a useful summary, not authority.
3. **Write the analysis before the conclusion.** If the reasoning doesn't hold up on the page, the conclusion is wrong.
4. **Say what would change the answer.** A memo that only states a conclusion isn't much use when the facts shift.
5. **Note what's still open.** Where the facts are incomplete, say what documentation is needed rather than assuming.

## A note on dates and amounts

Tax law changes. Dollar thresholds are indexed, and legislation moves the rules underneath them. Every figure in these memos reflects the law as I understood it at the date on the memo, and any real engagement would require verifying current amounts and guidance before a position is taken.
